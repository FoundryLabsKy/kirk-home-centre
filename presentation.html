01 · Where it started

The old site did its job for years. The web moved on.

Kirk earns 4.2 stars across 365+ Google reviews and is putting $3.5M into a bigger store. But someone finding you online met a single page built for a different era of the internet — while Uncle Bill's next door and A.L. Thompson's both run full online stores.

What visitors were running into

  • No padlock. Browsers now warn "Not secure" on pages like this — a rough first hello for a name people trust.
  • One page for everything. Nothing to browse, no departments, and almost nothing for Google to find.
  • Little things that read wrong. A header image that never loaded, and a stray symbol where "décor" should be.
  • An email that didn't match. Messages went to info@kel.ky — which looks like somebody else's address.
  • Hard to use on a phone. And a phone in the car park is exactly where people check your hours.

03 · What changed, and why

Every change started with something your customers told us

Nothing here is decoration. The reviews, the seasons, the neighbours' websites, the family history — each one shaped a decision. Here they are, side by side.

Change

HTTPS, mobile-first, one fast file

The whole site is a single self-contained page — no plugins, no build step — served over HTTPS on any static host.

Why

First impressions. The padlock is table stakes now, and Google quietly buries sites without it. One fast file also suits island connections — and never needs a specialist to look after it.

Change

Eleven departments as numbered aisle signs

The department system is styled as in-store overhead aisle signboards, and the homepage hero is the store directory.

Why

People love the store but say honestly that it's a squeeze in there. Until the expansion opens up the floor, the website can be the roomy way to browse — and Google now gets eleven pages to find instead of one paragraph.

Change

A Storm Prep hub with a seasonal banner

A dedicated hurricane page — impact windows, checklist, quote form — with a site-wide banner that switches itself on every 1 June and off after 30 November.

Why

You're the only place in Cayman for SIW and CWS impact windows — and every June the whole island starts thinking about them at once. The site now follows that rhythm on its own, no one has to remember to update it.

Change

A delivery promise page

Plain answers on what gets delivered, when it's scheduled, and exactly who to call if anything slips — plus a proposed written service standard.

Why

The sharpest words in your reviews are about deliveries that moved without a phone call. Saying plainly how delivery works — and who picks up when plans change — is the fastest trust you can win back.

Change

Heritage told properly

The Our Story page runs from William Kirkconnell's shipwreck before 1840, through the 1974 opening by Eldon and Charles Kirkconnell, to the $3.5M expansion approved in 2026.

Why

Six generations and a shipwreck is a story no competitor can buy, and Caymanians genuinely care about shopping local. The expansion makes it a story about the future too: bigger and better, same family.

Change

Real reviews, real names, verbatim

Testimonials quote actual Google and Facebook reviews word-for-word, with attribution, alongside the 4.2★ / 365+ review score.

Why

"Home Depot meets Bed Bath & Beyond" wasn't our line — a customer wrote it. Real people, real names and real words persuade in a way polished marketing copy never will.

Change

Contractor Sales gets its own front door

A dedicated page with the 7 AM desk hours, a three-step materials-list process, and a trade enquiry form.

Why

Builders were your first customers in 1974 and they still show up at 7 AM sharp. They needed their own front door, not a corner of the homepage — and your competitors already give them one.

Change

Structured data and one canonical NAP

HardwareStore schema markup with the exact address, split hours and brands, and one consistent name, phone and opening time everywhere.

Why

Around the web, directories still can't agree on your name, your phone number or when you open. The site now states the facts once, in a format Google reads directly — the anchor for tidying up everything else.

Change

Quote forms instead of dead ends

Storm-prep, contractor and general enquiry forms with visible labels and inline validation, composing a pre-filled email until a form backend exists.

Why

Windows and materials lists are conversations, not checkouts. Every page now ends with an easy way to start one — call, visit, or send a quick note — instead of a fax number.

06 · The launch runway

What happens next, in order

  1. Your sign-off.

    You confirm the delivery promise wording, the aisle numbers and the awards line — and hand over the official logo files with your blessing to use them.

  2. Bring kirkhomecentre.ky home.

    Point the domain at the new site with the padlock on for good, and set up a proper @kirkhomecentre.ky inbox so quote requests land somewhere that feels like you.

  3. Get the whole internet telling one story.

    Claim the Google Business Profile, then tidy Ecay, Yabsta, Cayman.Directory, FindYello and Cybo — one name, one number, doors open at 7. Retire the old "True Value" listings.

  4. A morning with a photographer.

    The storefront, the departments, the brands you're proud of — the layout already has places waiting for the real thing.

  5. Watch, then decide on online ordering.

    See how people actually use the site for a season. If the browsing says they'd buy, Phase 2 adds a searchable flyer — and a full shop only if it earns its keep.
